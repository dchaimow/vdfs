vdfs · equi-volume depth test on synthetic surfaces

Does the vdfs equi-volume formula preserve volume?

A comparison of the equi-volume area models of vdfs 2.0 (linear and quadratic) with the formula of vdfs ≤ 1.1, and with LayNii's voxel-based equi-volume layering. Depths are computed for synthetic white and pial surfaces with known geometry and checked against the exact equi-volume depths and by counting voxels per depth bin.

Denis Chaimow · 21 October 2026 · vdfs 2.0 (development) · LayNii 2.9.0 (523058c) · voxel size 0.125 mm · fourth version

Correction to the first version of this report. The first version built the LayNii rim incorrectly: it labelled voxels outside the grey matter as borders (LayNii expects the outermost grey matter voxels), and in the voxel boxes used for vdfs it decided the side of each border voxel from the exact geometry instead of the mesh. This put false pial border voxels right next to the white surface and produced plateaus in LayNii's depths, which the first version wrongly attributed to the ribbon being cut by the volume boundary. It also ran LayNii only with its default smoothing. All LayNii results below use a corrected rim and two smoothing levels; the section on how LayNii was run describes the procedure and its sensitivity.

Summary

  • A quadratic area model makes vdfs' equi-volume depths exact. Using the area of the midthickness surface (the average of white and pial vertices) in addition to the white and pial areas, the cross-sectional area of each column is modelled as quadratic in depth. Its depth errors (rms 0.0003–0.002, max 0.004) are as small as the error from approximating the surfaces by triangles, and its voxel counts match those of the exact depths in every patch.
  • The linear area model (Waehnert et al., 2014; surface_tools) preserves volume closely, but not exactly. Voxel counts per depth bin stay within 0.2–1.0 percentage points of the ideal 10%, with rms depth errors of 0.0004–0.015. In the cylinder it is exact up to the triangle approximation (rms 0.001); in spheres and strongly curved parts of the folded sheet a small error remains, because there the area grows quadratically with depth, not linearly as the formula assumes.
  • The formula of vdfs ≤ 1.1 was close but systematically off. Every curved geometry shows the same pattern: too many voxels at mid-depth and too few near the white and pial surfaces. Its rms errors are 1.6–4.5 times larger than those of the linear area model, with maximum errors up to 0.050 in the folded-sheet trough. Its mean error is near zero, which is why the results looked plausible.
  • Both are far better than no correction: equi-distance depths deviate by up to 6.4 percentage points per bin and 0.20 in depth.
  • LayNii's equi-volume layering is less accurate than the vdfs equi-volume variants and depends strongly on its settings. With default smoothing (100 iterations) its rms errors are 0.013–0.088. With 1000 iterations and a ribbon that is not cut near the evaluated region, it reaches 0.014–0.018 in the concentric shells, but in the folded sheet it corrects only part of the equi-distance bias (rms 0.043 at the crest, 0.074 in the trough, no correction at the saddle). Near a cut through the ribbon (small voxel boxes) its correction largely fails.
  • A vdfs robustness gap showed up: voxel centres lying exactly on a boundary between prisms can get no depth. It only happens with exact alignments, such as symmetric synthetic meshes.

The two formulas

For an equi-volume depth β (the fraction of the local cortical volume between the white surface and the point), vdfs needs the distance fraction α at each vertex, given that vertex's area on the white surface (aw) and on the pial surface (ap). The intermediate surface at depth β passes through white + α · (pial − white) at every vertex.

The formula in vdfs ≤ 1.1 (since the 2021 MATLAB prototype):

α = (2 a_p β − a_p β² + a_w β²) / (a_p + a_w)

The linear area model (the surface_tools formula, converted to depths measured from the white surface), available in vdfs 2.0 as area_model="linear":

α = (−a_w + √((1 − β) a_w² + β a_p²)) / (a_p − a_w)

surface_tools assumes that the area changes linearly with distance between the white and pial surfaces, and its formula is the exact equi-volume solution under that assumption. The formula of vdfs ≤ 1.1 was the algebraic inverse of surface_tools' function. surface_tools uses the letters the other way round (α for volume, β for distance) and measures from the pial surface, and inverting the formula turned "volume in, distance out" into "distance in, volume out". Measuring from the other surface largely compensates for this: the two formulas agree to first order in the area difference, but not in the second-order term.

The linear-area model goes back to Waehnert et al. (2014), whose Eq. (10) is the formula above (with α for volume and ρ for distance). They modelled cortical segments in 3D as truncated cones with end areas derived from both principal curvatures of the white and pial surfaces, and then assumed that "the area grows linearly with the height of the segment column", noting that this "describes an approximation of the actual cortical shape" and that it "balanced robustness, accuracy and precision best" among the models they tried and gives a closed-form solution.

The charts show how far each formula moves the intermediate surface away from the equi-distance position (α − β), for a vertex whose pial area is 3 times or one third of its white area.

An exact area model for vdfs

vdfs builds intermediate surfaces by moving each vertex along the straight line from its white to its pial position. A triangle whose vertices move linearly has an area that is quadratic in the distance fraction α (exactly so when the white and pial triangles are parallel):

A(α) = a_w (1 − α)² + 2 a_m α (1 − α) + a_p α²

The middle coefficient follows from the area amid of the midthickness surface, the linear morph at α = 0.5: am = 2 amid − (aw + ap)/2. The linear model is the special case amid = (aw + ap)/2. The volume fraction below α is then a cubic in α, which vdfs solves with a few Newton steps. For a sphere am = √(aw ap) and the model reproduces the exact volume; for a cylinder amid is the average and it reduces to the linear area model. On a real FreeSurfer subject, amid deviates from (aw + ap)/2 by −11% to +5% (5th–95th percentile of vertices), so the quadratic term matters in real cortex. FreeSurfer's own ?h.area.mid cannot be used for this: recon-all computes it as (area + area.pial)/2.

Test setup

Geometries and exact depths

  • Sphere, gyral crown: white r = 4 mm, pial r = 7 mm (pial/white area 3.06).
  • Sphere, sulcal fundus: pial r = 3 mm inside, white r = 6 mm outside (area ratio 0.25).
  • Cylinder, crown: white r = 4 mm, pial r = 7 mm (area ratio 1.75). Area grows linearly with radius, so the linear area model should be exact.
  • Folded sheet: white surface z = 2 cos(kx) cos(ky) with 20 mm wavelength; the pial surface is its parallel surface 3 mm along the normal. It contains a crest, a trough, a flank and a saddle.

Columns are radial in the shells and follow the white-surface normals in the folded sheet. Along a normal, the area changes by 1 − 2Ht + Kt² (H, K: mean and Gaussian curvature at the white surface), so the exact equi-volume depth of a point at distance t is V(t)/V(3 mm) with V(t) = t − Ht² + Kt³/3; in the shells it is (r³ − rw³)/(rp³ − rw³) (spheres) or the squared version (cylinder).

Spheres are meshed as an icosphere (2562 vertices), the cylinder and sheet as regular grids with 0.3 mm spacing. vdfs' equi-distance depths agree with the exact ones to within 0.0026, which bounds the error from approximating the surfaces by triangles.

Methods and checks

  • vdfs (2.0 development version) with equi-distance depths and both equi-volume area models (linear, and quadratic using the midthickness areas), and for comparison a copy of vdfs with the formula of vdfs ≤ 1.1 in alpha_from_beta. Vertex areas are calculated from the surfaces.
  • LayNii LN2_LAYERS -equivol with 100 (default) and 1000 smoothing iterations, in a large voxel box in which the ribbon is not cut near the evaluated patches (see below).
  • Voxel grid: 0.125 mm, so 24 voxels across the cortex, shifted by an irregular offset so that no voxel centre lies on a prism boundary.
  • Patches are sets of whole columns: a 25° cone in the spheres, a 25° wedge in the cylinder, and columns starting within 1.5 mm of the crest, trough, flank and saddle in the folded sheet. All methods are evaluated on the same patches.
  • Voxel counting: equi-volume depths put 10% of a patch's voxels into each tenth of the depth. With the exact depths the counts come out at 10.0 ± 0.5% (trough patch) or better, so the test resolves differences of about half a percentage point.

How LayNii was run

Rim

LN2_LAYERS grows distances through the voxel grid from its border voxels and computes the depth as dinner / (dinner + douter), measured between border-voxel centres. In its source code the border labels mark grey matter voxels (rim == 2 // WM boundary voxels within GM). The rim was therefore built as:

  • 3: grey matter voxels (centre inside the ribbon),
  • 2: grey matter voxels with a face neighbour outside the grey matter, on the white side (equi-distance depth < 0.5),
  • 1: the same on the pial side.

The edge of the volume does not count as a border. Grey matter and sides come from one source only: the exact geometry in the large box, and the vdfs equi-distance depths (consistent with the mesh) in the vdfs boxes.

Command and output

LN2_LAYERS -rim rim.nii -nr_layers 10 -equivol \
    -incl_borders -iter_smooth 100 -output ln2_s100
LN2_LAYERS -rim rim.nii -nr_layers 10 -equivol \
    -incl_borders -iter_smooth 1000 -output ln2_s1000

-incl_borders gives every grey matter voxel a depth (border voxels get 0 or 1), so all methods are evaluated on the same voxels. The continuous *_metric_equivol.nii output is used, oriented so that 0 = white. LayNii's equi-distance depths agree with the exact ones to rms 0.014–0.015 (max 0.04–0.045, at the border voxels, which are set to exactly 0 and 1).

How LayNii computes equi-volume depths (from LN2_LAYERS.cpp): every grey matter voxel is assigned its nearest inner and outer border voxel ("anchor"); voxels sharing an anchor form a column. For each column, LayNii counts the fraction w of its voxels in the inner equi-distance half, a voxel-counted estimate of the volume fraction at mid-depth, smooths w over the grey matter (-iter_smooth), and maps each voxel's equi-distance depth α to β = wα / (wα + (1 − w)(1 − α)). This curve is exact at both surfaces and at mid-depth and implies an area that varies as 1/((1 − w) + (2w − 1)α)². With an exact w, its largest depth errors would be 0.012 (sphere crown), 0.018 (sphere fundus) and 0.004 (cylinder), similar to the linear-area model; LayNii's larger errors in this test come from estimating w by counting about 24 voxels per column, from the smoothing needed to suppress that noise, which also blurs w across changes in curvature, and from columns cut by the volume boundary.

Large box: complete sphere shells, a cylinder 16 mm long (patches end 6.5 mm before its cut ends), and a folded sheet 34 × 29 mm (columns of the patches about 10 mm from the box faces).

LayNii: sensitivity to rim, smoothing and box size

Rim construction and smoothing (closed shells)

Three ways to build the rim from the surfaces: borders on the voxels just outside the grey matter ("outside", as in the first version of this report), on the outermost grey matter voxels ("inside", LayNii's convention, used for all other results), or on the voxels whose centre lies within half a voxel of the surface ("centred", which places the reference positions on the surface on average). The rim construction shifts LayNii's reference surfaces by up to half a voxel and changes the equi-distance error by a factor of about 1.7; the smoothing matters more for equi-volume: 100 iterations, tuned for about 0.2 mm voxels, leave grid-dependent errors at 0.125 mm. With a centred rim and 1000 iterations, LayNii reaches rms 0.007–0.009 in the shells. These shells favour strong smoothing, because their equi-volume correction is the same everywhere; in the folded sheet, more smoothing helps less (summary table).

vdfs: voxels exactly on prism boundaries

In vdfs ≤ 1.1, voxel centres lying exactly on a boundary between prisms could get no depth: rounding in the depth calculation placed them marginally outside every adjacent intermediate triangle. With a voxel grid aligned to the meshes' symmetry planes, 689 ribbon voxels of the sphere crown had no depth. vdfs 2.0 accepts points within a tiny tolerance of a triangle edge (#10); on the same aligned grid, – ribbon voxels are now without depth. Real surfaces practically never align like this, but synthetic or grid-aligned meshes can.

Conclusions

  1. The formula of vdfs ≤ 1.1 was not a different valid model but an inversion of the surface_tools formula. It gave systematically wrong equi-volume depths, with errors up to about 0.03–0.05 at strongly curved cortex. vdfs 2.0 corrects it.
  2. The quadratic area model, using the midthickness area as a third area per vertex, gives exact equi-volume depths for the vdfs prism geometry at no extra input cost and is the default in vdfs 2.0. The linear area model (area_model="linear") is its special case and roughly halves the errors of the vdfs ≤ 1.1 formula; the quadratic model removes them.
  3. Voxels exactly on prism boundaries now get a depth (#10).
  4. LayNii's equi-volume layering is less accurate in these tests and sensitive to the rim construction, the smoothing and cuts through the ribbon near the region of interest; vdfs is not affected by any of these. In the folded sheet LayNii corrects only part of the equi-distance bias even with strong smoothing.

Reproduction

All scripts are in the validation/ folder of the vdfs repository; run_all.sh <results dir> <LN2_LAYERS> runs everything and builds this page (see its README). vdfs ran with 10 parallel jobs; the four geometries took 13–150 s per variant.
